# DUKA SMART ELECTRONICS
## Business Insights & Analytical Findings

### 1. Executive Summary

This project analyses retail sales and customer transaction data for Duka Smart Electronics using PostgreSQL.

The analysis focuses on understanding customer purchasing behaviour, transaction patterns, payment methods and sales performance.

Using SQL techniques, the project addresses 46 business questions designed to extract meaningful information from the retail database.

The ultimate goal is to translate transactional data into actionable insights that can help management make informed business decisions.

### 2. Dataset Overview

The dataset contains 950 records, comprising:

| Metric | Total |
|---|---:|
| Customers | 150 |
| Orders | 800 |
| Total Records | 950 |
| Business Questions | 46 |

The analysis uses customer and transaction information to investigate different aspects of retail performance.

### 3. Key Analytical Findings

#### 3.1 Customer Analysis

Customer analysis helps establish how customers interact with the business.

The analysis investigates:

- Customer purchasing frequency.
- Customers with the highest order volumes.
- Customer spending patterns.
- Customer distribution by location.
- Differences in purchasing behaviour.

**Business Implication:**

Understanding customer behaviour enables the business to identify valuable customers and develop strategies that encourage customer retention and repeat purchases.

#### 3.2 Sales and Revenue Analysis

Revenue analysis provides an understanding of the financial performance of the business.

The analysis focuses on:

- Total sales revenue.
- Revenue generated from completed orders.
- Average transaction values.
- Sales performance across different periods.
- High-value and low-value transactions.

**Business Implication:**

Revenue analysis can help management evaluate sales performance, identify opportunities for growth and make better pricing and inventory decisions.

#### 3.3 Payment Method Analysis

The payment analysis examines the different payment methods recorded in the database.

One confirmed finding from the analysis is:

**11 orders were paid through M-Pesa.**

**Business Implication:**

M-Pesa is represented in the business's payment transactions. Management should compare its transaction volume and value against other payment methods before determining customer payment preferences.

#### 3.4 Order Analysis

Order analysis examines transaction activity and purchasing patterns.

The analysis investigates:

- Total orders placed.
- Order status distribution.
- Completed and non-completed transactions.
- Customer order frequency.
- Transaction patterns.

**Business Implication:**

Monitoring order activity can help management understand operational performance and identify potential challenges in order fulfilment.

#### 3.5 Product and Pricing Analysis

Product analysis examines pricing information and purchasing activity.

The analysis considers:

- Product prices.
- Product purchasing patterns.
- High-value and low-value products.
- Quantity purchased.
- The relationship between pricing and sales performance.

**Business Implication:**

Understanding product performance can help management make informed decisions about pricing, stock management and product availability.

### 4. Business Recommendations

Based on the analytical objectives, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. **Customer Retention:** Develop customer loyalty strategies to encourage repeat purchases.
2. **Revenue Monitoring:** Regularly monitor completed-order revenue to evaluate business performance.
3. **Payment Flexibility:** Continue monitoring payment methods to understand customer preferences.
4. **Inventory Management:** Use purchasing trends to guide stock replenishment and product availability.
5. **Sales Monitoring:** Track sales performance over time to identify growth opportunities.
6. **Data Quality:** Regularly validate customer and transaction records to maintain reliable reporting.
7. **Customer Segmentation:** Group customers according to purchasing frequency and spending behaviour to support targeted marketing.

### 5. Limitations

- The findings are based on the available dataset.
- The analysis depends on the accuracy and completeness of the recorded transactions.
- Some business conclusions require comparisons across multiple SQL query results.
- The findings should not automatically be generalised to other retail businesses.

### 6. Conclusion

The Duka Smart Electronics project demonstrates the importance of data analytics in understanding retail business performance.

Through PostgreSQL, transactional data can be transformed into meaningful information that supports customer analysis, revenue monitoring, sales evaluation and operational decision-making.

The project also demonstrates practical SQL skills, including data exploration, filtering, aggregation, joins, subqueries, CTEs and window functions.

**Overall, the analysis highlights the role of data-driven decision-making in improving business efficiency and supporting sustainable retail growth.**
